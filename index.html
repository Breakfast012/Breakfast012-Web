About Jiayi Wu (@Breakfast012)

LONDON, UK · TENCENT IEGG · UAL · HKUST · UCL MPBE

Jiayi Wu (吴嘉怡, @Breakfast012) is a London-based Technical Artist, Mocap & Rigging Pipeline Engineer, and Embodied Media Researcher working at the intersection of 3A character animation pipelines, biomechanical motion capture refinement, immersive XR systems, and sensor-based interaction.

At Tencent Interactive Entertainment Group (IEGG, London), she engineers production optical and inertial motion capture workflows (Vicon, Xsens, Faceware), cross-skeleton retargeting, skeletal and facial rigging (IK/FK, Swing-Twist, FACS BlendShapes), and Unreal Engine 5 C++/Python animation toolchains—achieving Rank #1 in performance evaluation across her graduate cohort. She is the creator of Motion Forge, a production UE5 C++/Python salience-guided animation and biomechanical mocap engine.

She graduated with First-Class Honours (14.3/15, Ranked 1st in Cohort) in BA (Hons) Immersive Media and Mixed Reality from University of the Arts London (UAL), and conducted Turing Scheme-funded visiting research at The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST), where she also served as a Unity real-time systems lecturer. Her academic and artistic work has been honored with the IEEE TVCG Best Paper Award (IEEE ISMAR 2026), two First-Author IEEE VRW 2026 papers, the Tom Burns Memorial Award 2025, the UAL People’s Prize for Equality and Diversity 2025, and the IEEE VR 2026 XR Gallery shortlist.

ARTIST & DESIGNER STATEMENT (ARCHIVE /CONTACT)
“Jiayi Wu is an interdisciplinary artist and designer working at the intersection of immersive media, sensor-based interaction, and social storytelling. Her practice explores how digital systems can reflect, challenge, and reframe emotional and bodily experience. Drawing from motion capture, VR, and haptic feedback, she develops interactive environments that respond to gesture, presence, and memory. Jiayi’s work is driven by a curiosity about how humans inhabit technological spaces, particularly in contexts shaped by constraint, disconnection, or resistance. Through each project, she asks how emerging media can expand our ways of sensing, sharing, and feeling together.”
